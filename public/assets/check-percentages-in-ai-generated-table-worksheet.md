## How to fill it in

1. Find the two counts behind the percentage: the part (numerator) and the whole (denominator). If the source does not give the denominator, write "denominator not stated".
2. Write the denominator in words, for example "all respondents" or "people who answered question 3".
3. Divide numerator by denominator and multiply by 100. Compare with the figure shown.
4. Read the label. Does it describe the same group as the denominator?

## Before you share the table

- [ ] I can name both counts behind every percentage.
- [ ] Every label says what the figure is a percentage of.
- [ ] People who did not answer are not counted as "no".
- [ ] A difference between two percentages is called percentage points. A relative change is (new − old) ÷ old.
- [ ] Groups such as "neither" or "not stated" are not merged into another group without saying so.
- [ ] No figure was changed to make a column total 100%. A rounded total of 99% or 101% has a note.
- [ ] Comparisons between periods use percentages with their own denominators, not raw counts from groups of different sizes.

## Limits of this check

Recalculating shows that the table matches the counts you were given. It does not show that the counts were collected or recorded correctly.
