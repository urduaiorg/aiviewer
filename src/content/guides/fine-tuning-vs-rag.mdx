If you want an AI to know about your company's proprietary data—like your internal HR policies, your codebase, or your customer service history—you cannot just use a standard, off-the-shelf model. It will hallucinate because it has never seen your data.

To make an AI "smart" about your specific world, the tech industry uses two primary methods: **RAG** and **Fine-Tuning**. Understanding when to use which is the most critical architectural decision an enterprise can make in 2026.

## What is RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)?

RAG is an **"Open-Book Test"** for AI.

Imagine asking a lawyer a highly specific question about a recent contract. Even a brilliant lawyer wouldn't answer from memory. They would go to their filing cabinet, find the specific contract, read it, and *then* answer your question.

RAG does exactly this. 
1. **Retrieval:** When you ask a question, the system searches your private database (often called a vector database) for the most relevant paragraphs of text.
2. **Augmentation:** It grabs those paragraphs and invisibly pastes them into the prompt alongside your question.
3. **Generation:** The AI reads the provided context and generates an accurate answer.

### When to use RAG
- You have massive amounts of data (thousands of PDFs or wikis).
- The data changes frequently (e.g., daily stock prices or inventory levels).
- You need the AI to cite its sources (e.g., "According to page 4 of the employee handbook...").

## What is Fine-Tuning?

Fine-Tuning is a **"Closed-Book Test"** for AI.

Instead of giving the AI a reference manual, you are actually putting the AI back into the classroom. You provide the model with hundreds or thousands of high-quality examples of how it should behave. The system then mathematically adjusts the internal "weights" (the actual brain of the AI) to match those examples.

Fine-tuning doesn't teach the AI a massive database of new facts very well. Instead, it teaches the AI a new *behavior*, *tone*, or *format*.

### When to use Fine-Tuning
- You want the AI to write in a very specific corporate brand voice.
- You need the AI to consistently output a complex, strict JSON format for a software application.
- You want to use a smaller, cheaper AI model but train it to perform one specific task as well as a massive, expensive model.

## Why Not Both?

In 2026, the most sophisticated enterprise AI systems don't choose between the two—they combine them. 

A company might **fine-tune** an open-source model so that it perfectly understands medical terminology and responds with a compassionate bedside manner. Then, they will use **RAG** to feed that fine-tuned model the specific patient's medical history right before it generates a response. 

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is RAG cheaper than fine-tuning?
Yes, generally. RAG relies on standard API calls and vector database storage, which are relatively cheap. Fine-tuning requires renting expensive GPU compute time to actually retrain the model's weights.

### Does fine-tuning prevent hallucinations?
No. In fact, fine-tuning an AI on new facts can sometimes increase hallucinations because you are asking the AI to rely on its internal "memory" rather than looking up a concrete source document. RAG is the best defense against hallucinations.

### What is a Vector Database?
A vector database is a specialized storage system used in RAG. Instead of storing text as letters and words, it converts sentences into "vectors" (lists of numbers) that represent the *meaning* of the text, allowing the AI to search for concepts rather than exact keyword matches.

### How many examples do I need for fine-tuning?
While older models required tens of thousands of examples, modern models in 2026 can often be effectively fine-tuned with as few as 50 to 100 high-quality, diverse examples (a process sometimes called Few-Shot Fine-Tuning).
