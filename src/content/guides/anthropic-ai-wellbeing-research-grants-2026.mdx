## What was announced

Anthropic announced a $5 million program on August 25 for independent research evaluating AI's effects on wellbeing. The stated support includes funding, model access and technical assistance, with open-source results. Its announcement says selected applicants would be notified by October 5 to submit full proposals. That is a planned notification date, not evidence of awards already made.

The announcement emphasizes clear pass/fail definitions, expert involvement, realistic multi-turn scenarios, and checking automated graders against subject-matter experts. The $5 million figure describes the total program pool; it does not establish an individual award amount.

## A reusable way to read an evaluation proposal

The following is an **authored proposal-reading exercise**, not a submitted application or clinical guidance. Compare two fictional descriptions:

> A: We will make an AI assistant safer and ask users whether they like it.

> B: We will specify one behavior to evaluate, explain what counts as an error, have qualified reviewers assess the same cases, and report disagreements between those reviewers and the automated grader.

Description B gives a reader more of the evaluation process to inspect. It still leaves questions unanswered: how cases are selected, whether they represent intended use, who the qualified reviewers are, and how uncertainty will be reported. A detailed method is a starting point for review, not proof that the evaluation is valid.

Use a small claim ledger when assessing a future funding call:

| Claim | Evidence to request or locate |
|---|---|
| The opportunity is open | Current deadline and official submission status |
| My institution is eligible | Stated eligibility, rather than a broad topic match |
| A particular award size is available | Per-project funding terms, distinct from the total pool |
| The proposed evaluation works | Defined cases, scoring rules and independent checks |
| Results can be shared | Actual publication, licensing and privacy terms |

Record missing information as unknown. Do not replace an absent award size with a plausible budget or assume that an interesting topic establishes eligibility.
