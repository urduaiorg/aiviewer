Microsoft made its bet early and loudly. In early 2023, it invested $13 billion in OpenAI, integrated AI into every product in its suite, and positioned Copilot as the AI assistant that would reach more people than anyone else — not because it was necessarily the best, but because it was already inside the tools that hundreds of millions of people use every day.

That theory of distribution was sound. The execution has not matched it. Here are the numbers.

## The Adoption Gap

**Web market share:** Copilot's standalone web product holds approximately 1.1% of the AI assistant market as of early 2026, down from 1.5% a year ago. ChatGPT holds roughly 60%. Google Gemini is at around 18%. Perplexity, Claude, and even Grok each hold larger shares than Microsoft's flagship AI product.

**Enterprise adoption:** Of Microsoft's 450 million business customers, approximately 15 million are paying for the 365 Copilot add-on. That is 3.3%. For a company with Microsoft's enterprise reach and sales infrastructure, this number is significantly below expectations.

**Subscriber trajectory:** The paid subscriber share for Copilot among U.S. AI subscribers dropped from 18.8% in July 2025 to 11.5% in January 2026 — a 39% contraction in market position in six months.

**Retention after trial:** Perhaps the sharpest data point. Around 70% of enterprise users initially chose Copilot — it is right there in the apps they already use, requiring no new workflow or download. After trying alternatives, only 8% continued choosing it. Something is not working in the product experience itself.

## What Went Wrong

### The assistant nobody needed to open

Copilot's distribution advantage is real: it is embedded in Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook, Teams, and the Windows taskbar. You do not need to go anywhere to find it. The problem is that "already there" is not the same as "useful enough to return to."

The research is consistent: users who encounter Copilot inside an existing app and use it for a task often find it capable of the task — but not capable enough to displace their existing habit of using a separate, better-regarded tool. You open Word for a document and use Claude for the writing. You open Excel for the spreadsheet and use ChatGPT for the analysis. Copilot's presence in the sidebar does not change the habit.

### The $30/month problem

Microsoft 365 Copilot costs $30 per user per month on top of existing 365 subscriptions. For enterprise buyers, this is a significant line item for a product with unproven ROI. ChatGPT Enterprise and Claude for Business offer comparable or stronger capabilities at similar or lower price points — and they are not bundled with a software suite that buyers are already locked into.

The bundle that was supposed to be Copilot's advantage — you already pay for Office, so adding AI is easy — is not playing out that way. The $30 add-on is evaluated on its own merits. On its own merits, it is losing.

### Model performance

For most of its life, Copilot ran on OpenAI's GPT-4o — which is a capable model, but not differentiated. Every other major AI product also had access to GPT-4o or a model at that tier. Microsoft's differentiation was supposed to come from integration, not the model. But integration without a product experience that is genuinely better than switching windows has not been enough.

Microsoft has responded by investing in its own in-house models — reportedly targeting readiness by late 2026. Whether those models can close the quality gap against Claude Opus 4.7, GPT-5.5, and Gemini 2.5 Pro is uncertain.

## What Microsoft Still Has

The failure to convert early advantages into usage is real, but writing off Microsoft is premature.

**Azure:** While Copilot struggles, Azure OpenAI Service is a dominant infrastructure layer for enterprise AI deployments. Microsoft may not win at the consumer AI assistant level, but it is making significant revenue from companies that build AI on its cloud. These are separate businesses with different competitive dynamics.

**GitHub Copilot:** This product — the code completion tool inside VS Code and JetBrains — has fared significantly better than its Office counterpart. With the SpaceX/Cursor deal raising questions about Cursor's independence, GitHub Copilot could see renewed interest from developers who want a model-agnostic tool without ownership complexity.

**The installed base:** 450 million business users is not a number that evaporates. If Microsoft ships a meaningfully better Copilot experience in the second half of 2026 — whether through its own models or a revised product design — the distribution advantage becomes real again. The problem is time. Habits are forming around competitors right now.

## The Honest Assessment

Microsoft is not losing the AI race in the way a company losing a race loses — falling further behind with each mile. It is more that it is running alongside the leaders with a far larger engine and failing to accelerate past them.

The numbers suggest a product that is present but not preferred. That is a harder problem than being absent — it means the users are aware of it, tried it, and chose something else. Fixing a product people tried and left requires a more substantial change than acquiring new users for the first time.

The market is still early enough that this is recoverable. But the window is closing faster than Microsoft's current pace of improvement suggests it realizes.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Is Microsoft Copilot free?**
There is a free version of Copilot at copilot.microsoft.com, but it uses a limited model. Microsoft 365 Copilot — the version integrated into Word, Excel, Outlook, and Teams — costs $30 per user per month on top of an existing Microsoft 365 subscription.

**How does Copilot compare to ChatGPT?**
Both use versions of GPT models under the hood. ChatGPT on a Plus or Pro subscription ($20–$200/month) consistently scores higher in user satisfaction and retention data. The main advantage Copilot offers is integration directly into Microsoft Office apps, which eliminates switching between tools for users already embedded in that ecosystem.

**Is GitHub Copilot the same as Microsoft Copilot?**
No. GitHub Copilot is a separate product focused on code completion inside code editors — VS Code, JetBrains, and others. It is significantly more widely used and better regarded than the Microsoft 365 Copilot. Both are Microsoft products but serve different audiences and work differently.

**What is Microsoft doing to fix Copilot?**
Microsoft has acknowledged the underperformance internally (described by some reports as a "Code Red"). The company is developing its own in-house AI models intended to reduce dependence on OpenAI and potentially offer differentiated capabilities. A significantly revised Copilot experience is expected in late 2026.
