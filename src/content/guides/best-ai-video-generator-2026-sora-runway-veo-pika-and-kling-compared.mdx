Compare AI video generators on a shot you actually need. Define what would make the clip usable, generate a small fixed batch and count rejected attempts. This gives you a decision grounded in your project instead of a ranking based on selected demos.

## Write a shot brief before choosing the tool

Our fictional exercise is a five-second clip for a pottery workshop page. The desired shot shows a blue mug on a wooden table while the camera slowly moves closer. The handle must remain attached, the mug must keep its shape and color, and there must be room for a title added later in an editor.

These are acceptance conditions rather than promises about what a model will produce. Before spending credits, check that your chosen product supports the required duration, aspect ratio, reference inputs and export format in your account. Save the relevant documentation and check date in your trial notes.

## Keep the scene and motion simple

An example prompt for the exercise is:

> A blue ceramic mug rests on a wooden table beside a window. The camera slowly moves closer. Soft daylight, steady continuous movement, one uninterrupted shot.

If you use an input image, make sure you have permission to use it. Record whether the tool received that image, text alone or other controls. Those differences affect what you are comparing.

For one concrete documentation example, [Runway's Gen-4 prompting guide](https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/39789879462419-Gen-4-Video-Prompting-Guide) recommends simple prompts and describes the roles of subject, camera and scene motion. Its guidance is specific to that workflow; check the instructions for the model you actually use.

## Reject against the brief, then assess style

Use the same checks for every exported clip:

| Check | Pass condition for the pottery exercise |
|---|---|
| Subject identity | The mug stays blue and retains its handle |
| Shape | The mug does not visibly stretch or melt during the shot |
| Camera | Movement is a continuous slow approach |
| Composition | The intended title area remains usable |
| Export | The file opens in the editor at the required size and duration |

A clip that fails an essential condition is rejected even if its lighting looks impressive. Style preferences can decide between clips that already meet the brief. This prevents attractive but unusable output from receiving the highest score.

Watch at normal speed, then inspect the beginning, middle and end. A still frame cannot establish consistency across the whole shot. Record the reason for each rejection so a later prompt revision has a specific target.

## Worked example: the cheaper attempt may cost more

The following numbers are **fictional teaching data**, not current provider prices or measured tool results.

| Trial | Attempts | Total generation cost | Usable clips | Cost per usable clip |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 6 | CAD 3.00 | 2 | CAD 1.50 |
| B | 6 | CAD 4.80 | 4 | CAD 1.20 |

Divide generation cost by usable clips: 3.00 ÷ 2 = 1.50, and 4.80 ÷ 4 = 1.20. Trial B has a higher generation bill but a lower cost per usable clip in this invented example. If a trial yields zero usable clips, report that result directly; do not divide by zero or hide the failures.

Keep editing time separate. Four clips needing substantial repairs may be less useful to your project than two that are ready to assemble. Subscription commitments, taxes and unused credits are also separate from this simple variable-cost calculation.

## Run a comparison you can explain

Start each candidate with the same creative brief, aspect ratio and planned attempt budget. Where interfaces differ, document the differences rather than pretending their controls are identical. Keep the first fixed batch as your baseline.

If you revise the prompt, record the change and treat that as another round. Do not compare one tool's first attempt against another tool's tenth refined attempt without explaining the imbalance. Save prompts, source inputs, exported clips, rejection reasons and actual credit use.

A small trial supports a narrow statement such as “this workflow produced two usable pottery clips under these conditions.” It does not justify saying one product is the best for all subjects, animation styles or budgets.

## Your exercise

Use the mug brief or substitute one object you can legally depict. Write three non-negotiable conditions and one optional style preference. Complete a trial record, select a clip and explain why the rejected clips failed.
