## Identify the problem before the plan

Write one sentence: “I need to complete ___, but my current setup prevents ___.” Examples include a file-size limit on documents you regularly process, an unavailable feature or a capacity limit that interrupts a time-sensitive task.

Then check whether the proposed plan addresses that precise limitation in your region and account. Do not treat paid access as unlimited. As one documented example, [OpenAI's Plus help page](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/6950777-what-is-chatgpt-plus) explains that usage limits can apply and that API usage is billed separately from the ChatGPT subscription.

## Keep a task log

For one typical week, record the task, whether you finished, whether you hit a limit and how much active checking or repair was required. Define “useful” before counting: a draft you discarded is an attempt, not a completed result.

## Calculate cost per useful result carefully

Suppose a hypothetical plan costs CAD 24 per month and you repeat this exact fictional week four times. You would have 32 attempts and 24 useful results. Cost per useful result would be CAD 24 ÷ 24 = **CAD 1.00**.

Dividing by all 32 attempts gives CAD 0.75, which answers a different question. Neither figure measures the quality of the result or includes your review time. The four-week assumption is a simplification, not a claim that every calendar month contains exactly four weeks.

If you only completed six useful tasks over the entire billing period, the cost per useful result would be CAD 4.00. Occasional use can produce a different decision from frequent use even at the same subscription price.

## Test the claim that matters

If your reason to upgrade is fewer interruptions, record interruptions during a bounded trial. If it is better document handling, use representative documents you are allowed to share and check the results against their sources. Keep the input and acceptance conditions consistent when comparing options.

Include checking and correction time in any claim about time savings. An answer produced quickly may take substantial effort to repair. Do not count the model's self-reported confidence as a quality measurement.

## Decide before the next renewal

Use these questions:

- Did the plan address the original limitation under my actual account conditions?
- How many useful tasks did I complete, and what did they require me to check?
- Which paid features did I actually use?
- Does an existing tool or free workflow already cover this task adequately?
- What are the renewal amount, currency, taxes and cancellation terms shown in my account?

Choose to keep, cancel or continue a bounded trial based on the record. This worksheet does not choose a provider for everyone or claim that a subscription will create income.
