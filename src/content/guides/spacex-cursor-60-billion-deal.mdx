On April 21, 2026, SpaceX announced something unusual: it had secured the option to acquire Cursor — the AI code editor built by Anysphere — for $60 billion later this year. If SpaceX ultimately walks away from the full acquisition, Cursor still collects $10 billion. Either way, the collaboration between SpaceX and Cursor has already started.

For the millions of developers who use Cursor daily, the question is simple: what does this mean for the tool you rely on?

## What Actually Happened

The deal has two parts, and keeping them separate matters.

**The collaboration:** SpaceX and Anysphere are jointly building what they describe as a next-generation "coding and knowledge work AI." This work runs on Colossus, SpaceX's supercomputer cluster with compute equivalent to one million Nvidia H100 GPUs. This part is already in motion.

**The option:** At some point later in 2026 — after SpaceX's planned IPO on Nasdaq — SpaceX will either acquire Anysphere (Cursor's parent company) for $60 billion, or pay $10 billion and walk away. The timing is tied to the IPO because SpaceX wants to finance a $60 billion acquisition using publicly traded stock rather than restructuring its private finances.

What SpaceX paid for is not Cursor itself — it is the right to decide. The $10 billion is the option premium. Cursor already banked it.

## Why SpaceX, Not xAI?

This is the question most people are asking. Elon Musk has an AI company — xAI, which makes Grok. Why is the deal through SpaceX instead?

The answer appears to be compute and timing. SpaceX owns Colossus, the supercomputer cluster. xAI also uses Colossus, but the infrastructure asset sits inside SpaceX. Running Cursor's next-generation model on Colossus requires a SpaceX deal, not an xAI one.

There is also a competitive framing issue: xAI competes directly with the OpenAI and Anthropic models that Cursor currently offers. Cursor's value proposition is that developers can use any frontier model — GPT-5.4, Claude Opus 4.7, Gemini. An outright xAI acquisition would raise immediate questions about whether Cursor stays model-agnostic or becomes locked to Grok. SpaceX as the acquiring entity provides a degree of separation, at least structurally.

Worth noting: two of Cursor's most senior engineering leaders left Anysphere to join xAI before the deal was announced, reporting directly to Musk. The organizational lines between SpaceX, xAI, and now Cursor are blurrier than the corporate structure suggests.

## Cursor's Trajectory That Made This Possible

The $60 billion figure only makes sense in the context of how fast Cursor has grown.

- January 2025: valued at $2.5 billion
- May 2025: valued at $9 billion
- November 2025: $29.3 billion post-money valuation after a $2.3 billion Series D
- February 2026: annual run rate reached $2 billion
- April 2026: SpaceX option values the company at $60 billion

That is a 24x increase in valuation in 15 months. Cursor's run rate is projected to reach $6 billion by the end of 2026. At that scale, $60 billion is roughly 10x annualized revenue — expensive, but within the range of how high-growth AI infrastructure companies are being priced.

## What Changes for Cursor Users Right Now

The short answer: nothing, yet.

Cursor is still built by the same team at Anysphere. The same models are still available. Pricing has not changed. The collaboration with SpaceX is focused on building a new product on Colossus, not modifying the existing Cursor product.

The things that could change, and when:

**Model availability.** If the full acquisition closes after the SpaceX IPO, the question of whether Cursor stays model-agnostic becomes real. A Cursor owned by SpaceX/xAI might favor Grok over Claude and GPT. No announcements have been made, and Anysphere's founders have not addressed this publicly.

**Pricing.** An acquisition at $60 billion requires a return. Companies acquired at large multiples tend to either monetize more aggressively or expand upmarket into enterprise. Cursor's individual subscription is currently $20/month, which is relatively modest for a tool at its usage scale.

**Competition.** GitHub Copilot (Microsoft), Claude Code (Anthropic), and OpenAI Codex are all accelerating. A Cursor backed by Colossus compute could produce a significantly more capable model than any competitor. That is likely the strategic argument SpaceX is making internally.

## Should Developers Be Worried?

The honest answer is: it is too early to know, but the risk is real.

Cursor's biggest strength is that it is not owned by any of the foundation model companies. It can be genuinely model-agnostic — offering Claude, GPT, Gemini, and open-source models without a conflict of interest. The moment it becomes part of Elon Musk's broader technology portfolio, that neutrality becomes harder to maintain, even if the company structures it carefully.

The $10 billion collaboration — regardless of whether the acquisition closes — already represents a significant commitment to the SpaceX/xAI orbit. How that affects roadmap decisions, model partnerships, and product direction over the next 12 months is the real question.

For now: Cursor still works the same way it did last week. But this is worth watching closely.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Has SpaceX completed the Cursor acquisition?**
No. SpaceX paid $10 billion for the option to acquire Cursor for $60 billion later in 2026. The full acquisition is expected after SpaceX's planned IPO this summer. If SpaceX chooses not to acquire, Cursor keeps the $10 billion and the companies' collaboration continues.

**Will Cursor still support Claude and GPT models after acquisition?**
No announcements have been made about model availability changes. Anysphere has not publicly addressed whether the acquisition would affect model-agnosticism. This is one of the most important open questions for Cursor's current users.

**Why is the deal through SpaceX and not xAI?**
SpaceX owns the Colossus supercomputer that the collaboration will run on. There is also a strategic reason: structuring the deal through SpaceX rather than xAI preserves some appearance of separation from Grok, which would compete with the OpenAI and Anthropic models Cursor currently offers.

**What is Cursor's current valuation?**
The SpaceX option values Cursor (Anysphere) at $60 billion. In November 2025, it was valued at $29.3 billion in its Series D round. Its annual revenue run rate was $2 billion in February 2026.
