**Use Gemini's new small-business tools to organize a question, then check the underlying records before using its answer.** Google announced a business landing page on **October 20, 2026**, alongside marketing and finance skills, brand-style generation and new app connections. Google says the hub and connections are *beginning* a global rollout to Gemini app users; they may not yet appear in every account. [Google's announcement](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/new-tools-small-business-owners/)

Google says Gemini can turn spreadsheets, receipts and purchase records into financial graphics and interactive models. That can make a messy file easier to discuss. A polished chart, however, cannot establish that its inputs are complete, that its arithmetic is right or that an ad campaign caused a change in sales. This lesson uses a small fictional ledger to practise those checks. It is **not** an AIViewer test of Gemini's output.

## A fictional cafe ledger

**All figures below are invented for teaching.** Imagine a cafe comparing one ordinary week with one promotion week. These are the complete inputs for the exercise; there is no actual Gemini output or measured campaign result.

| Measure | Ordinary week | Promotion week | Source meaning |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Sales revenue | $1,200 | $1,650 | Recorded sales before the cafe's cost of goods |
| Gross margin assumption | 40% | 40% | Assumed share of sales left after the cost of goods |
| Marketing spend | $100 | $250 | Total spend in each week |

An **authored, deliberately flawed interpretation** is: “The campaign made $450 extra profit because sales increased from $1,200 to $1,650.” The $450 is the difference in *revenue*. Calling it profit ignores the margin and the $150 increase in marketing spend. The two-week comparison also cannot prove that the campaign caused the sales change.

## Recalculate before making a chart

| Check | Calculation | Result |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Revenue difference | $1,650 − $1,200 | **$450** |
| Gross contribution, ordinary week | $1,200 × 40% | **$480** |
| Gross contribution, promotion week | $1,650 × 40% | **$660** |
| Gross contribution difference | $660 − $480 | **$180** |
| Marketing-spend difference | $250 − $100 | **$150** |
| Difference after these two inputs | $180 − $150 | **$30** |

The last row is a **descriptive difference**, under the stated 40% margin assumption. It is not full net profit: rent, wages, discounts, taxes and other costs are absent. It is not campaign return on investment either, because weather, holidays, price changes and other factors might explain some of the sales difference. A chart titled “Campaign earned $450 profit” would therefore be wrong; “Sales rose $450 between these two weeks” is supported by the supplied records.

## A bounded request and a better decision note

If the finance feature is available in your account and the records are approved for use, give Gemini a narrow request:

> Use only this two-week ledger. Show sales, assumed 40% gross contribution, marketing spend and the difference after marketing spend for each week. Show every formula and its units. Label this as a descriptive comparison, not proof that the promotion caused the change. List missing costs and other explanations before recommending any action. Do not modify an ad campaign, connected file or account.

A **corrected authored decision note** would read: “Sales were $450 higher in the promotion week. Under a constant 40% gross-margin assumption, gross contribution rose $180 while marketing spend rose $150, leaving a $30 difference after those inputs. We do not know the full profit or the promotion's causal effect. Check the actual margin, discounts and comparable weeks before raising the budget.”

Try the exercise yourself: if promotion-week sales were **$1,500** instead of $1,650, with every other input unchanged, what would the final difference be? **Answer:** promotion-week gross contribution becomes $600; the contribution difference is $120; subtracting $150 extra marketing spend gives **−$30**. That sign change shows why a small input error matters more than a chart's visual polish.

For a real decision, compare Gemini's cited input rows with the records, verify formulas and units, check permissions on connected accounts, and have the responsible person approve any action.
